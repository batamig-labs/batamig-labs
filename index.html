What I do

  • Write what is hard to write

    Incident-response guidance, migration journeys, API and CLI references. I work docs-as-code, in Markdown and Git. In my years at Microsoft, I wrote more than 2,000 merged pull requests on Microsoft Learn, spanning both quick fixes and extensive, cross-service content overhauls.

  • Govern the knowledge layer

    Even when agents do the drafting, someone has to own what is true. I set up review so that agents propose, writers approve, and the source stays canonical.

  • Direct AI-assisted content work

    I set direction for documentation agents, for tests of how well docs serve AI answers, and for ticket-driven doc fixes.

  • Lead content teams

    I coach and grow writers. My last role was leading a team of writers across Microsoft Defender services. Throughout my entire career, I’ve mentored junior writers and other people entering or pivoting into tech.

Working together

Where an AI pipeline needs a human editor

AI can do correctness. Taste, judgment and ownership of what is true still need a person. These are the shapes that work usually takes.

  • Documentation projects

    A defined piece of hard-to-write content: a migration journey, incident guidance, a content set that needs restructuring, or a content journey that needs new direction.

  • Process and program leadership

    Moving work forward across teams I don’t manage: priorities, stakeholder updates, written decisions, and follow-through.

  • Strategy sprints

    A short, focused engagement to define content strategy for AI-generated documentation: what the pipeline covers, who reviews it, and how you know it is working.

Selected work

Projects I led or owned

Migration

One path for two merging products

Situation
As Microsoft Sentinel moved into the Microsoft Defender portal, the guidance customers needed was all gated, scattered, and confusing.
What I did
I consolidated information from many stakeholder teams into one article that customers could follow and took it public with the product preview.
Outcome
The customer experience team lead cited it as the most requested content from their customers during the initial migration period.

Incident response

Customer guidance after a major supply-chain attack

Situation
After a widely publicized nation-state attack, customers needed clear steps to understand their exposure and mitigate it.
What I did
I was asked to write the article and owned it through months of review by legal, product, and threat-intelligence teams. It had to pull guidance from across Microsoft Security services into one piece that covered both prevention and recovery. I worked with security researchers and legal reviewers who rarely deal with documentation. The topic was sensitive, and every statement had to hold up.
Outcome
Published guidance that customers around the world used to respond.

Acquisition

Documentation through an acquisition

Situation
A newly acquired Operational Technology security product had to fit into its new parent company’s platform and documentation standards, with a unique set of customer audiences and needs.
What I did
As the sole documentation owner, I restructured the enterprise product docs, created an online catalog for supported hardware devices, rebuilt API and CLI references, and created self-service deployment procedures for user personas who were accustomed to more handholding. In this role, I also served as a design partner on the Microsoft Learn PDF refresh effort, arguing for the offline PDFs our users relied on.
Outcome
A fully integrated set of content that both met unique customer needs and clicked into the enterprise standards and processes.

Documentation revamp

AlgoSec: from PDFs to an online help system

Situation
AlgoSec’s documentation was managed in a legacy system and delivered to customers as PDFs. The company was growing, and it was still working out what dedicated technical writing could contribute and whether its docs should be public.
What I did
As the sole full-time writer, I owned the docs end-to-end and managed subcontractors for the migration and lower-priority work. I moved the content into MadCap Flare and published an online help system with product and version selectors, and made the case for publishing the docs publicly. I kept the internal knowledge base current, tied the docs’ branding and language to the marketing materials so customers recognized what they had bought, and set up Jira-based content tracking and Google Analytics reporting.
Outcome
Support efforts were smoother, and by the time I left, technical content had a recognized place in the company, with a brand and direction of its own for my successors. The online help’s main structure, selectors included, is still recognizable years later.
