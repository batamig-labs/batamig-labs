I’m Batami Gold. I’ve got two decades of experience writing and managing documentation, with the last six at Microsoft and seven in security.

These days, I focus on the knowledge behind the docs, drafting the hard parts myself. I build processes around that knowledge: who reviews, who’s informed, and how we know it reaches people.

I want what’s true, current, and trusted to be the source that both readers and AI tools draw on.

I’m open to both full-time roles and project work, in documentation leadership and technical content, in Israel or remote in EMEA.

What I do

Bring me in for

  • Writing what’s hard to write

    Incident-response guidance, migration journeys, onboarding guides, and references. I work docs-as-code, in Markdown and Git, or in any tools you prefer. My work spans both quick fixes and extensive, cross-service content overhauls.

  • Governing the knowledge layer

    Even when agents do the drafting, someone has to own what’s true. I set up review so that agents propose, humans approve, and the source stays canonical.

  • AI-assisted content work

    I set direction for documentation agents, for tests of how well docs serve AI answers, and for ticket-driven doc fixes. I champion AI-native practice and use AI daily to draft, review, measure, and report on content.

  • Keeping people aligned through change

    Migrations, acquisitions, deprecations, and incidents all need one clear account of what’s happening, what it means, and what to do next. I write it, get it through legal and product review, and keep stakeholders informed along the way.

  • Running content projects end to end

    Planning and tracking content work so it lands with the release. I’ve run documentation backlogs in Azure DevOps and Jira, set priorities with product and engineering, and managed subcontractors through full content migrations and complicated projects.

  • Leading content teams

    I coach and grow writers. My last role was leading a team of writers across Microsoft Defender services. Throughout my career, I’ve mentored junior writers and other people entering or pivoting into tech.

Working together

Where an AI pipeline needs a human editor

AI can do correctness. Taste, judgment, and ownership of what is true still need a person. These are the shapes that work usually takes.

  • Documentation projects

    A defined piece of hard-to-write content: a migration journey, incident guidance, a content set that needs restructuring, or a content journey that needs new direction.

  • Process and program leadership

    Moving work forward across teams I don’t manage: priorities, stakeholder updates, written decisions, and follow-through.

  • Strategy sprints

    A short, focused engagement to define content strategy for AI-generated documentation: what the pipeline covers, who reviews it, and how you know it’s working.

Selected work

Projects I’ve owned

Migration

One path for two merging products

Situation
As Microsoft Sentinel moved into the Microsoft Defender portal, the migration guidance customers needed was gated, scattered, and confusing. Without one trusted walkthrough, users would get stuck mid-migration.
Audiences
Customers needed one path to follow. Support and customer experience teams needed one trusted link to send them.
What I did
I consolidated information from many stakeholder teams into one article that customers could follow and took it public with the product preview.
Outcome
The customer experience team lead cited it as the most requested content from their customers during the initial migration period.

Acquisition

Documentation through an acquisition

Situation
A newly acquired operational technology (OT) security product had to fit its new parent’s platform and documentation standards. Its docs were fragmented across systems, blocking customer trust and onboarding.
Audiences
OT teams used to vendor handholding needed self-service deployment steps. Enterprise security teams needed docs that matched the rest of the platform.
What I did
I restructured the onboarding and usage docs, built a catalog of supported hardware appliances, rebuilt the API and CLI references, and wrote an end-to-end, self-service deployment process. I also championed PDF quality and design for a Microsoft Learn PDF refresh.
Outcome
A fully integrated content set that met its customers’ unique needs and fit the enterprise standards.

Org change

Guiding a writing org into knowledge engineering

Situation
A 50-person documentation org was shifting from traditional technical writing to AI knowledge engineering, and no formal function existed yet to lead the change.
Audiences
Writers and leads needed to know what was changing in their work and where to find guidance. Leadership needed the change to keep moving.
What I did
I led the internal virtual team that carried the change in the meantime, authored its charter, and landed our guidance in the org wiki ahead of an all-hands.
Outcome
The guidance was in place and findable before the all-hands, and the virtual team kept the change moving until a formal transformation group was created.

Internal communications

A newsletter program with four series

Situation
The org needed a regular channel for news and guidance, and there was no way to tell whether anyone read what was sent.
Audiences
Stakeholders needed a consistent set and style of updates. I needed to know which of those updates reached them.
What I did
Over the course of two years, I ran the newsletters across 30 editions and four series, and introduced view tracking where none existed.
Outcome
Metrics I was able to take to both content and stakeholder leadership: about 7,600 views across 30 editions.

Documentation revamp

From PDFs to an online help system

Situation
AlgoSec delivered its docs as PDFs from a legacy system. Content was hard to search and update, and customers struggled to find answers that should have been self‑service.
Audiences
Customers needed easy answers. Support needed docs to link to. Leadership needed to see what dedicated technical writing could add.
What I did
I migrated the docs to an online help system, complete with advanced product and version selectors. I also set up content tracking and analytics, and reported on the metrics to leadership and stakeholders.
Outcome
Technical content earned a recognized place in the company, and the help system’s structure is still recognizable years later.
